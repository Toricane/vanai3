Synthesis: Across clusters, the most commonly hoped-for positives are in science and research (including disease research, physics, and innovation), education, and the automation of repetitive administrative or bureaucratic tasks. Some see gains in business efficiency, manufacturing/production, IT/communications, entrepreneurship, the financial sector, and the distribution of information; a few mention personal uses such as self-help, motivation, and companionship, and potential improvements in navigating professional services. Counterbalancing these are strong reservations: multiple respondents said “None/No benefit/Nowhere,” warned of job loss, misinformation, hallucinations, unsafe/untested systems, negative mental health impacts, exploitation of artists, and political risks. Overall, participants envision conditional benefits in targeted domains if human oversight and responsible deployment are prioritized, while noting deep skepticism about whether that is how AI will actually be used.
